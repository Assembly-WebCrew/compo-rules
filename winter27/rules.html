General rules

These general rules apply to all competitions. Compo-specific rules override them where applicable. If in doubt, contact CompoCrew at compo-contact@assembly.org or on the #demoscene channel of the Assembly Discord server.

  • Entries are submitted at scene.assembly.org, which is published approximately two weeks before the event. There you can also find the full schedule of deadlines and juries. All deadlines are hard - contact the organizer if you're stuck.
  • Whether remote entries are allowed depends on the specific competition's rules. A ticket is not required for a remote entry but is required to vote.
  • By default, desktop entries run on the compo PC. If your entry needs a desktop platform we do not provide, for example macOS, a specific GPU, or a specific Linux setup, you may bring your own machine and we will capture the entry from it, provided you arrange this with the compo crew before the deadline and the machine outputs 1080p60 over HDMI.
  • A valid IBAN is required to receive money prizes. Money prizes are paid afterwards, so you do not need to be present at the party. Only a physical trophy requires the author or a designated proxy to be present at the prize giving.
  • Submitting an entry means accepting the competition contract.
  • Maximum one entry per artist per competition. If you submit multiple entries, only one will be accepted.
  • Every entry and its main creative effort must be previously unreleased. Realtime entries may include music and graphics that compete at the same event. Previously released components, such as fonts, system samples, and licensed libraries, are allowed only when they are not the main effort and you have the rights to use them. After an entry has been shown in the competition, you may release or exhibit it.
  • AI-generated content is not allowed unless the competition's rules explicitly permit it. This applies to the entry's own content: code, graphics, and music. Tools used to make the entry, such as synths, trackers, packers, editors, and compilers, are fine even if AI was used to develop those tools.
  • You must own or have permission for all material in your entry. The burden of proof is on you. Trademarked or copyrighted content, including logos, is not allowed without permission. Finnish copyright law has no "fair use". See On Copyright at the end of this page for what this means in practice.
  • Fan art using copyrighted characters or works is not allowed. With money prizes at stake, this is enforced strictly.
  • Every competition has a time slot, which determines how many entries can be shown. If more entries are submitted than fit, a preselection jury chooses which entries are shown.
  • Entries that are not shown are not released by Assembly, so they are not counted as released and may be entered at another party.
  • Organizers may disqualify explicit, racist, or disturbing material, modify rules to clarify ambiguities, or change prizes, especially if entry quality or number is low.

Capture assistance

Entries are shown on stage from recordings. Entries that run on the compo PC are captured by the compo crew as part of the normal competition process.

If your entry runs on anything else - your own machine, a console, retro hardware, or another platform - you are primarily expected to record the entry yourself and submit the video. We can help with capturing at CompoBase, but this is assistance rather than a guaranteed service. Agree on it with the compo crew well before the deadline and state in the submission notes what your entry needs to run, including the platform, browser, cables, and audio output. If the device has no external audio output, deliver the soundtrack separately.

Combined Demo

Refer to the General rules with the following additions:

This competition celebrates creative real-time audiovisual demos. If you can code it and it runs on a publicly available platform, it belongs here. Beginner entries participate under the same rules as all other entries.

Entries may target any generally available platform, including but not limited to:

  • Modern desktop platforms such as Windows, macOS, and Linux.
  • Retro and oldskool platforms such as C64, Amiga, Atari ST, NES, SNES, Mega Drive, and Game Boy.
  • Fantasy consoles and virtual platforms such as PICO-8, TIC-80, Picotron, Quadplay, Voxatron, and IBNIZ.
  • Other accessible environments such as WebGL, DOS, Java, or similar software environments.

If your entry requires custom or one-off hardware, such as an FPGA, microcontroller, or mechanical device, submit it to Wild (with AI) instead.

  • Realtime entries may include music and graphics that compete at the same event.
  • Commercial and non-commercial engines, frameworks, libraries, plugins, and other production tools may be used. Previously released components and assets, such as fonts and system samples, are also allowed when they are not the main creative effort and you have the right to use them.
  • List third-party material and significant external technology used in the production in the entry form or accompanying readme. Creative Commons and Public Domain material must include a citation and a link to the source.
  • Generative AI may assist with programming, including agentic coding. The entry must not be primarily generated by AI: its main creative and technical contribution must come from the participating artist or group.
  • If generative AI is used directly to create the submitted entry, enable the Gen AI option in the entry form and briefly describe how it was used. AI-generated graphics, music, audiovisual material, and other creative assets may not be used as content in the entry.
  • Engines and other production tools are allowed even if generative AI was used to develop them. Using such a tool does not make the entry AI-generated, and the Gen AI option is not required unless generative AI was used directly to create the submitted entry.
  • Fan art using copyrighted characters or works is not allowed.
  • The entry must be executable. Pure video playback, rendered animations, and executables that only contain an animation and a player are not allowed.
  • The production must run in real time and show in a similar manner each time it is executed.
  • The production may use temporary files where the platform provides them but must not permanently modify system settings.
  • The demo may be up to 8 minutes long, but organizers may stop it before the end.
  • The compressed submission archive may be at most 2 GB. Keep the size reasonable on other platforms too.
  • A screenshot must be submitted with the entry.
  • Submit the executable and all other files needed to run it, plus a readme with platform and launch instructions.
  • Entries that do not run on the compo PC must include a high-quality video capture, preferably 1080p60. Real-hardware capture is preferred, but emulator capture is acceptable. See Capture assistance in the general rules.
  • Entries can be tested at CompoBase or by prior arrangement with the compo crew. Arrange testing early enough to fix any problems.
  • Browser-based entries are accepted. They are run in a mainstream desktop browser on the compo PC - Chrome by default. State in the submission notes if the entry needs Firefox or another browser. The entry must be fully self-contained as a single file, with no external resources loaded from the network. Browsers differ, so test the entry on the compo machines. Tools such as WebDemoExe can be used to package a browser demo into a more controlled runtime environment.
  • The entry must be launchable using the normal method for its platform, such as an executable, cartridge, disk image, or web player.
  • Hide the mouse cursor where applicable.
  • The user must be able to exit at any time using the platform's standard quit method, such as ESC on a PC, where the platform supports this.
  • Windows intros should avoid importing by ordinal from DLLs with non-fixed ordinals.
  • Size-limited intros are welcome. The platform and size will be shown on the information slide.

Remote entries are welcome.

Combined Graphics

Refer to the General rules with the following additions:

Make the graphics you have always wanted to make using the platform and technique of your choice. Modern digital art, pixel art, 3D rendering, and oldskool platform formats all belong in this combined competition.

  • All entries must be original. No covers, copies, or reproductions of someone else's work.
  • If you use Creative Commons, Public Domain, or permitted stock material, declare it in the entry form, cite the source, and follow the licence. The major part of the entry must be your own creative work.
  • AI-generated content is not allowed. The entry's content must be human-made; tools are covered by the distinction in the general rules.
  • Fan art using copyrighted characters or works is not allowed.
  • Submit the display version as an RGB PNG or JPEG. CMYK images are not supported.
  • Entries made in oldskool or other native formats must also include a converted PNG or JPEG for judging and display. State the platform and native format in the submission notes.
  • The maximum display resolution is 1920x1080 or 1080x1920. Portrait entries will appear smaller because of letterboxing.
  • Submit a version of the final image with no visible signature, using _nosig in its filename. Entries without an unsigned version will be disqualified. A signed version may also be included.
  • Include a readme.txt describing the techniques and tools used and identifying the sources and licences of any third-party material.
  • Clear rule violations result in disqualification; resubmission will not be requested.
  • Entries are presented anonymously until voting has closed. Do not put an artist or group name in the title, filename, technical details, or submission notes.

Remote entries are welcome.

Combined Music

Refer to the General rules with the following additions:

Make the track you have always wanted to make using the platform and technique of your choice.

  • Your entry must be your own work. Covers, remixes, and rearrangements are not allowed. Copyrighted samples and other copyrighted material are not allowed unless you have a licence covering this use, such as a Creative Commons licence or sample-library licence.
  • If you use Creative Commons or Public Domain material, declare it in the entry form and cite the source.
  • AI may not be used to compose or generate the music. A track or part of a track produced by prompting is not allowed. Using AI as part of the production process, such as an AI-generated vocal sound for your own lyrics and composition, is acceptable if you have the rights to the generated material and follow the other rules. Tools such as synths, trackers, DAWs, and effects are fine even if AI was used to develop them.
  • You may not use music for which you have not acquired the necessary rights. Most published music is controlled by Teosto or Gramex and is therefore not usable. You are liable for infringements.
  • There are no restrictions on composing techniques. Modern DAWs, trackers, oldskool platforms, and other methods are allowed.
  • Supported delivery formats are 320 kbps MP3, FLAC, and WAV.
  • Entries made on oldskool platforms or in tracker formats must also be converted and submitted in one of the supported delivery formats.
  • A preselection jury will select entries to fit the competition time slot, typically 45 minutes. Entries that are not shown are handled as described in the general rules.
  • For playback, all entries are converted to FLAC and ReplayGain volume control is applied. Normalising the volume is recommended; excessive compression gives no advantage.
  • The entry must be no longer than 3 minutes 30 seconds. Exceeding the limit results in disqualification rather than a fade-out.
  • Entries are presented anonymously until voting has closed. Do not put an artist or group name in the title, filename, technical details, or submission notes.

Remote entries are welcome.

Photo

Refer to the General rules with the following additions:

  • Submit the displayed image as an RGB PNG or JPEG at exactly 1920x1080. CMYK images and portrait orientation are not supported.
  • Only minor retouching and adjustments are allowed:
    • cropping and straightening
    • lens correction and scaling
    • color-temperature and exposure adjustment
    • desaturation
  • Do not add or remove elements from the picture.
  • Composite and multiple-exposure photographs are not allowed. The photo must be taken with a single exposure; this excludes internal photo stacking and HDR.
  • Automatic non-generative camera processing is allowed only when it does not combine multiple exposures or generate, add, or remove image content.
  • AI-generated content is not allowed. Generative filling, replacement, and other generated image content are prohibited.
  • Submit the photo without a watermark or visible signature and include _nosig in the filename.
  • If the displayed photo has been adjusted, also submit the untouched original at its native resolution. Camera-original formats, including RAW, DNG, and HEIC, are accepted, as are JPG and PNG.
  • Original files and optional behind-the-scenes material are used only for verification and will not be distributed.
  • For a scan of a photograph or slide, retain the physical original and provide evidence or present it to the jury if requested.
  • Entries are presented anonymously until voting has closed. Do not put an artist or group name in the image, filename, title, technical details, or submission notes.
  • Clear rule violations result in disqualification; resubmission will not be requested.

On Copyright

Every year a few entries run into copyright trouble, almost always by accident. This section explains where the line is. If your case is not covered, ask before the deadline at compo-contact@assembly.org or on the #demoscene channel of the Assembly Discord server. Asking is free.

Why we are strict. Assembly pays real prize money, entries are recorded and stay online for years, and Finnish law has no "fair use". A detail that passes unnoticed during the compo is still searchable a decade later. The burden of proof is on you: if we have to ask, you need to show where the material came from and what allows you to use it. An entry we cannot clear is not shown.

The basics. Copyright applies automatically. No copyright notice does not mean free, being publicly available does not mean free, and there is no minimum length below which copying is safe.

Other people's material. Samples of released recordings, covers, remixes, and rearrangements all need permission from the rights holder, and we do not allow them. Permission from the author personally is not enough if they belong to a collecting society such as Teosto or Gramex, because they no longer administer those rights themselves. Sample libraries sold to be used in music are fine; recordings sold to be listened to are not.

Creative Commons, public domain, and stock. These are allowed where the competition's rules permit, on two conditions: follow the licence and declare it in the entry form with a link to the source. Undeclared licensed material is treated as unlicensed. Note that NonCommercial licences are not safe here, because Assembly is a commercial event that pays prize money.

Using licensed third-party material does not break the "previously unreleased" rule. That rule is about your own work: the entry and its main creative effort must not have been released before. A licensed font or instrument library does not make your entry released as long as it is not the main effort. If you are unsure which side of that line you are on, ask before the deadline.

Trademarks and logos. These are not allowed, not even as a retro homage, and this includes redrawn versions. Trademarks are protected separately from copyright, and entries are archived and re-shown afterwards. Evoke the era, hardware, and palette instead: none of those belongs to anyone.

Fan art. This is not allowed, however well made. A practical test: if an average viewer would name the source when looking at your entry, it is fan art; if they would only name a style or mood, it is inspiration. A painting in the style of a known illustrator with your own subject is fine. A painting of a named character from a game or film is not. For borderline cases, send us a description or a work-in-progress shot and we will answer in writing. Do not rely on parody; Finnish law gives it far less room than some other countries.

If something is wrong. The entry is disqualified and not shown. If the problem surfaces afterwards, the result can be revised and prize money withheld. Document your sources in the entry form and in a readme: what the material is, where it came from, and under what licence. Entries with clean documentation almost never become a problem.

Finnish rights organisations: Teosto and Gramex (music), Kopiosto (publications), Kuvasto (visual arts), and IFPI Finland (recordings).
